Mutation stacking, explained simply

A mutation is a modifier applied to a plant, and each mutation in the calculator's database of 158 mutations has its own multiplier. Stacking means applying more than one mutation to the same plant. The total is not just the largest mutation — it is the combined effect of the whole stack, applied to the plant's base value, weight, and tier.

Why order and labelling matter

Some entries in the database are marked reference only. Those entries are informative labels that may not be mapped to the active formula. They are shown for awareness, not guaranteed multiplier maths. If you count them as formula multipliers, your total will be too high, and a trade based on that total will look better than it really is.

A safe stacking workflow

  1. List every mutation on the plant, exactly as shown in the game.
  2. Enter them in the calculator one by one, checking the total after each one.
  3. Note which ones are reference only, and keep them separate in your head from formula multipliers.
  4. Compare the stacked total with the unmutated value, so you can see the stack's real contribution.
  5. Save the result before you change anything else.

When a stack looks too good

If a stack gives a total far above what you expected, check three things first: weight, tier, and reference-only labels. One of those three is usually the explanation. Game updates can also change multipliers, so a stack that was accurate last month may need a data refresh. Values are estimates for planning, not official prices.
