Trade Values (WFL) — Win, Fair, or Lose?

Last updated: 4 November 2026. WFL is a simple verdict for a trade: Win means your side is worth more than what you give, Fair means both sides are close, and Lose means you are overpaying. This page shows how to get that verdict with the calculator on this site, instead of guessing from a single plant's name.

How to Check a Trade in 5 Steps

  1. Calculate your side first. For each plant, enter exact weight, fruit tier, and mutations in the calculator, then save it as a line item. Do not round weight.
  2. Calculate their side the same way. Use the same method, same tier rules, and same mutation handling. A trade is only comparable if both sides use the same maths.
  3. Add the line totals. The list total on each side is your comparison number, not the single most expensive plant.
  4. Check amount and friend boost. Amount and boosts change a line total, and they are easy to forget when you focus on one plant.
  5. Decide WFL. If your side's total is clearly higher, it is a Win. If both totals are close, it is Fair. If your side is lower, it is a Lose — walk away or renegotiate, even if the other side looks shiny.

Three Traps That Flip a WFL Verdict

  • Reference-only modifiers: counting them as guaranteed multipliers makes a side look better than it is.
  • Rounded weights: a small rounding error, multiplied by a large mutation stack, becomes a large error.
  • Saved lines do not auto-update: each saved line stores the value at the moment you added it. If you change a setting later, re-add the affected lines instead of assuming the list updated itself.

Fruit Tier Check

Before a WFL call, confirm tier: Rainbow and Diamond ×50, Gold ×20, Silver ×5, other tiers ×1. Tier alone can turn a Fair trade into a Win or a Lose, so never skip it. For the full multiplier list, see Mutations.

When in Doubt, Don't Rush

For a small trade, a quick check is enough. For a large trade, do the full workflow: calculate, save, review, compare, then decide. If a deal looks too good, check weight, tier, and reference-only labels first — one of them is usually the explanation. Values here are planning estimates from bundled data, not official in-game prices.
