Raised-Bed Soil Mix

A practical raised-bed soil mix: topsoil, compost and drainage, with ratios, settling and what to avoid.

A starting mix

A common starting mix for a raised bed is about 60% topsoil and 40% compost by volume, with drainage improved by the bed's open bottom — not by a gravel layer inside the bed, which can perch water. For heavy clay underneath, loosen the native soil before filling.

Settling

Fresh mix settles, often by 10–20% after watering. Fill to the top, water well, and top up before planting. Test your soil if you are unsure about pH or nutrients — a mix alone does not fix a nutrient problem. Use the soil volume calculator for quantities.

A mix that works in most gardens

A common raised-bed mix is about 60% topsoil, 30% compost and 10% aeration material such as perlite or coarse vermiculite, by volume. The topsoil gives structure and minerals, the compost gives nutrients and water-holding capacity, and the aeration material keeps the mix from compacting. If your topsoil is already sandy, reduce the aeration material; if it is heavy clay, increase compost and aeration. There is no single perfect ratio — the test is whether water drains through in minutes, not hours, and whether the mix stays moist for a day after watering.

Step by step: filling a new bed

  • Line the bottom only if needed: cardboard on grass suppresses weeds and breaks down; landscape fabric is unnecessary in most beds and can block worms.
  • Fill in layers: add topsoil and compost in alternating layers, watering lightly between layers to settle them.
  • Leave 1–2 inches at the top: for mulch and to stop soil washing out when watering.
  • Water and top up: water well, wait a day for settling, then top up to the final level before planting.

Mistakes to avoid

Filling a bed entirely with compost sounds generous but backfires: pure compost holds too much water, shrinks dramatically as it breaks down, and lacks the mineral structure roots need. Using only garden soil from elsewhere in the garden compacts and may bring weed seeds. Buying "fill dirt" instead of topsoil gives you subsoil with almost no organic matter or nutrients. Check what you are actually buying before it is tipped on your driveway.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I refresh the mix?

Top up with compost each season, 1–2 inches dug lightly into the surface. A full replacement is rarely needed unless the mix has compacted badly or become diseased.

Can I use the mix for containers too?

It is heavier than potting mix and drains less freely, so it suits large raised planters but not small pots. For pots, use a proper potting mix — see the container soil calculator.
