Paver Calculator

Calculate how many pavers you need for a patio or path from area and paver size, including 10% waste for cuts and breakage.

Paver Calculator

Formula

Pavers = area (sq ft) ÷ (paver size in feet)², rounded up. With waste: pavers × 1.10, rounded up. Example: 100 sq ft with 12-inch pavers needs 100 pavers, or 110 with 10% waste.

How to use it, step by step

Measure the patio or path area in square feet, including the full paved area but not the edging. Enter the paver size as the side of a square paver in inches. For rectangular pavers, use the equivalent square size that gives the same area per paver, or calculate pavers per square foot from the paver's own area. Always order the waste allowance — cuts at edges, corners and around drains use more pavers than a clean rectangle suggests.

Worked example in detail

A 10 ft × 10 ft patio is 100 square feet. With 12-inch (1 ft) pavers, that is 100 pavers, 110 with waste. With 6-inch pavers, the same patio needs 400 pavers, 440 with waste — small pavers look good but need four times as many units and much more laying time. That trade-off is worth knowing before you choose a size for looks alone.

What affects real paver needs

Pattern, edge cuts, and breakage all increase the count. A herringbone pattern wastes more than a simple running bond. Irregular shapes, circles and curves waste more than rectangles. Keep a few spare pavers after the job for future repairs — matching the same batch and colour later is often impossible, so spares are worth more than the few dollars they cost now.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include joint gaps?

No — it assumes pavers laid edge to edge. Wide joints (over about 0.25 inch) reduce the count slightly, but the waste allowance usually covers the difference. For precise jointed layouts, ask your supplier for a layout calculation.

What about a base layer?

Pavers need a compacted base of gravel and sand underneath, which is a separate volume calculation. Use the gravel calculator for the base layer volume.

Should I order extra beyond 10%?

For complex patterns or curved edges, order 15% extra. For a simple rectangle, 10% is usually enough.

Understanding your results

The result gives two numbers: pavers needed with no waste, and pavers with 10% waste for cuts and breakage. Order the waste figure, not the exact figure — a patio that runs out of pavers halfway through cannot be finished with a different batch, because colour varies between batches. Keep any spares after the job for future repairs.

How do I calculate pavers for a rectangular paver?

Use the paver's area: a 12 × 6 inch paver covers 0.5 sq ft, so divide your area by 0.5. The calculator assumes square pavers, so for rectangles, convert to the equivalent square size first.

Do I need a base under pavers?

Yes — a compacted gravel base and a sand bedding layer. Pavers laid directly on soil sink unevenly and move. Use the gravel calculator for the base volume.

What pattern wastes the least pavers?

A simple running bond wastes least. Herringbone and basket-weave patterns look good but waste more on cuts, so order 15% extra for those patterns.
