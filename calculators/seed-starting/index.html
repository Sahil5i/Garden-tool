Seed Starting Calendar Calculator

Calculate indoor sowing dates from your last frost date and weeks before transplanting. Harden seedlings off before planting out.

Seed Starting Calendar

How it works

Indoor sowing date = last frost date minus weeks before transplanting. Transplant date = last frost date itself, after hardening off. Example: last frost 15 April, 6 weeks before = sow indoors around 4 March.

Last frost dates are averages, not guarantees — check your local forecast and local extension frost dates, and protect seedlings if a late frost is forecast.

How to use it, step by step

Enter your last frost date and the weeks before transplanting from the seed packet — commonly 4–8 weeks for tomatoes, peppers and brassicas. The calculator subtracts those weeks to give an indoor sowing date. Sow in trays or pots with seed compost, keep them warm and evenly moist, and give strong light once seedlings emerge. A week before transplanting, harden seedlings off by increasing their time outdoors each day.

Worked example in detail

If your last frost date is 15 April and tomatoes need 6 weeks indoors, sow around 4 March. If you sow on 15 March instead, seedlings will be only 4 weeks old at transplanting — smaller, but often fine. Sowing too early is worse than sowing slightly late: leggy seedlings grown indoors for too long transplant poorly and may never catch up with later-sown plants.

What affects seed starting success

Light is the most common limiting factor indoors. A bright windowsill is often not enough in early spring, and seedlings stretch towards the light and become weak. If seedlings are leggy, they need more light, not more fertilizer. Temperature matters too: most seeds germinate best at 65–75°F (18–24°C), and cold soil slows germination even if the air feels warm.

Frequently asked questions

What is hardening off?

Gradually acclimatising indoor seedlings to outdoor sun, wind and temperature over about a week. Start with a few hours in shade, increase daily, and bring them in at night until nights are reliably frost-free.

Can I sow directly instead?

Yes for many crops — beans, peas, carrots and lettuce do well direct-sown after frost. Tender crops like tomatoes and peppers benefit most from indoor starting, because it gives them a longer season.

What if I miss my sowing date?

Sow anyway, a week or two late, and choose a faster variety if available. A late start is far better than no start, and many crops catch up in warm weather.

Understanding your results

The result gives two dates: when to sow indoors and when to transplant. The sowing date is the last frost date minus the weeks on the packet. If that date has already passed, sow now and accept slightly younger seedlings — they often catch up in warm weather, while sowing months early produces leggy plants that transplant poorly.

What if I do not know my last frost date?

Use your local extension service or a nearby weather station's average last frost date. It is an average, not a guarantee — in a cold year, frost can come later, so keep frost protection ready.

Can I start seeds earlier under grow lights?

Lights help with light, not with temperature or space. Seedlings started too early still become leggy and root-bound before transplanting, even under lights.

What does 'weeks before transplanting' mean on a packet?

It means weeks indoors before the transplant date, which is usually the last frost date for tender crops. Count back from that date, not from today.
