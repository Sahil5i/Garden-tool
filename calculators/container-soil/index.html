Container Soil Calculator

Calculate potting mix volume for a round pot from diameter and height. Tapered pots hold less than a straight cylinder.

Container Soil Calculator

Formula

Volume = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × height, in cubic inches, ÷ 1,728 = cubic feet. Example: 12 in × 12 in = about 0.79 cubic feet. Tapered pots hold less — treat this as an upper estimate and buy a little less if your pot narrows sharply.

How to use it, step by step

Measure the inside diameter and height of the pot in inches. The calculator uses radius (half the diameter) and the cylinder formula, then converts cubic inches to cubic feet by dividing by 1,728. For tapered pots, use the average diameter — halfway between top and bottom — as an approximation. Round up to the nearest bag size, and remember that drainage material at the bottom reduces the soil volume needed slightly.

Worked example in detail

A pot 12 inches across and 12 inches high has radius 6 inches. Volume = 3.14 × 6² × 12 = 1,357 cubic inches, or 0.79 cubic feet. That is just under a 1-cubic-foot bag. A 16-inch pot of the same height holds about 1.4 cubic feet. Pot volume grows with the square of the diameter, so a small increase in pot size needs a surprisingly large amount of extra soil.

What affects container soil choice

Use potting mix, not garden soil, in containers — garden soil compacts, drains poorly and can carry pests. Potting mix is lighter and drains better, but dries out faster, so containers need more frequent watering than beds. Larger pots hold moisture longer and need less frequent watering, which is why small pots are harder to keep alive in summer.

Frequently asked questions

Should I put gravel at the bottom?

No. Gravel does not improve drainage in a pot; it raises the water table inside the pot and reduces root space. Use a pot with drainage holes and a saucer you can empty.

How often should I replace container soil?

Most potting mix lasts one to two seasons before it breaks down and compacts. Top up annually and replace fully when plants stop thriving despite feeding and watering.

Can I reuse old potting mix?

Yes, if the previous plants were healthy. Mix it with fresh compost, remove old roots, and do not reuse mix from diseased plants.
