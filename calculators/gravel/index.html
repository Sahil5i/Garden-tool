Gravel Calculator

Calculate gravel volume in cubic feet and cubic yards for paths, driveways and drainage areas, from length, width and depth.

Gravel Calculator

Formula

Cubic feet = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 12. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. Example: a 10 ft × 4 ft path at 3 in deep needs 10 cubic feet, or 0.37 cubic yards.

How to use it, step by step

Measure the path or area, including any edging you will fill. Choose depth by use: 2–3 inches suits a garden path, while a driveway needs a deeper, compacted base layer plus a surface layer — this calculator gives the volume for one layer, so calculate base and surface separately if you are building a driveway. Order by cubic yards for bulk delivery, and ask the supplier for the weight in tons if your vehicle or driveway has a weight limit.

Worked example in detail

A path 20 ft long and 3 ft wide at 3 inches deep needs 20 × 3 × 3 ÷ 12 = 15 cubic feet, or 0.56 cubic yards. At 2 inches it needs only 10 cubic feet; at 4 inches, 20 cubic feet. Depth is the easiest number to get wrong, and a path that looks thin after spreading usually needed one more inch, not more area.

What affects real gravel needs

Compaction reduces volume after spreading and rolling, so add about 10% for a driveway base. Gravel migrates into soft soil without landscape fabric underneath, so fabric saves gravel over time on paths. Angular gravel locks together and stays put better than rounded pea gravel, which rolls and spreads. For drainage, use a coarser grade and a deeper layer, and never use gravel to hide a drainage problem that needs a proper outlet.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons is a cubic yard of gravel?

It varies by stone type, size and moisture, typically around 1.2–1.5 tons per cubic yard. Always confirm with your supplier, because ordering by tons from a volume figure you guessed is a common source of short deliveries.

Do I need landscape fabric under gravel?

For paths on soil, yes — it stops gravel sinking into mud and weeds coming up. For drainage trenches, use a permeable fabric that lets water through, not plastic sheeting.

Can I use this for a driveway?

For volume, yes, but a driveway needs a compacted sub-base as well as a surface layer. Calculate each layer separately and add them.

Understanding your results

The result is a volume in cubic feet and cubic yards. Suppliers sell gravel by weight (tons) as well as by volume, and the conversion varies by stone type, so use the volume to order and ask the supplier for the weight if you need it for a vehicle or a weight limit. If the result is under 1 cubic yard, bags from a garden centre may be simpler than a bulk delivery.

How deep should gravel be on a path?

2–3 inches over landscape fabric is enough for a foot path. Driveways need a deeper, compacted base layer plus a surface layer — calculate each layer separately.

Will gravel stop weeds completely?

No, but a 3-inch layer over fabric suppresses most weeds by blocking light. Seeds that land on top of the gravel can still germinate, so occasional weeding is still needed.

What is the difference between gravel and decorative stone?

Gravel is usually angular and locks together, which suits paths and driveways. Rounded decorative stone looks good in beds but rolls and spreads underfoot, so it is a poor path material.
