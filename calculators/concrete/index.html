Concrete Calculator

Calculate concrete volume in cubic feet and cubic yards for slabs, paths and shed bases, plus approximate 80 lb bag count.

Concrete Calculator

Formula

Cubic feet = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 12; cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. Bag count uses about 0.6 cubic feet per 80 lb bag — check your bag label, as yields vary by mix. Example: a 10 ft × 4 ft slab at 4 in deep needs 13.3 cubic feet, or 0.49 cubic yards, about 23 bags.

How to use it, step by step

Measure the slab area and the depth you plan to pour — 4 inches suits most garden paths and shed bases, while driveways need a deeper slab and reinforcement. Calculate volume first, then decide bags vs ready-mix: bags suit small jobs, ready-mix suits slabs over about 1 cubic yard, because mixing that much by hand is slow and the first batch starts setting before the last is poured.

Worked example in detail

A shed base 10 ft × 8 ft at 4 inches deep needs 10 × 8 × 4 ÷ 12 = 26.7 cubic feet, or 0.99 cubic yards — just under 1 yard, about 45 × 80 lb bags. Ordering ready-mix for just under 1 yard may incur a small-load charge, so compare the bag total (including your time) with the ready-mix delivered price before deciding.

What affects real concrete needs

Sub-base, formwork and waste all matter. A slab poured on uneven ground uses more concrete in low spots. Formwork that bulges under the weight of wet concrete also increases volume. Order about 10% extra for slabs, and have a use ready for any surplus — a small path or stepping stones — because wet concrete cannot be stored.

Frequently asked questions

How thick should a garden path be?

About 4 inches for foot traffic, with a compacted gravel base underneath. Thinner slabs crack under frost and root movement.

Bags or ready-mix?

Bags for under about 0.5 cubic yards, ready-mix above about 1 cubic yard. Between those, compare delivered price, your time, and whether you can pour and finish the whole slab before it starts setting.

Do I need reinforcement?

For a small shed base, usually not if the base is well compacted. For larger slabs and driveways, mesh or rebar helps control cracking — follow local building guidance for structural slabs.

Understanding your results

The result gives volume in cubic feet and cubic yards, plus an approximate bag count. The bag count uses about 0.6 cubic feet per 80 lb bag, which varies by mix — check your bag label. If the volume is just under 1 cubic yard, compare the bag total with a ready-mix delivered price, because a small-load charge can make ready-mix more expensive than bags.

How long does concrete take to set?

It starts setting within hours and is walkable in a day or two, but it keeps curing for weeks. Do not put heavy loads on a new slab for at least a week, and keep it damp in hot weather so it cures rather than drying out.

Do I need to seal a concrete slab?

For a garden path or shed base, usually not. Sealing helps in freeze-thaw climates and where oil or chemicals might spill, but it is not required for a basic slab.

Can I pour concrete in cold weather?

Only with care — concrete poured below about 40°F (4°C) may not cure properly. Cover it, use a suitable mix, and avoid pouring in frost. In most regions, spring to autumn is the safe window.
