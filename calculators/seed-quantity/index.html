Seed Quantity Calculator

Calculate seeds to sow from bed area and spacing, with a germination buffer. Sow extra, then thin to final spacing.

Seed Quantity Calculator

Formula

Plants = area ÷ (spacing in feet)². Seeds = plants ÷ (germination rate ÷ 100), rounded up. Example: 32 sq ft at 12 in spacing needs 32 plants; at 85% germination, sow about 38 seeds.

Germination falls with old seed and cold soil — if your seed is old, sow more, or test a few seeds on damp paper first.

How to use it, step by step

Enter the bed area in square feet and the spacing in inches from the seed packet — the calculator first works out how many plants fit in that area (area ÷ spacing in feet squared), then divides by the germination rate to give seeds to sow. Enter the germination rate from the packet as a percentage — if the packet does not give one, use 70% for fresh seed and lower for old seed. Then add a small buffer for thinning, pests and failed transplants, especially for crops sown directly outdoors.

Worked example in detail

Take a 32 sq ft bed at 12-inch spacing: plants = 32 ÷ 1² = 32 plants. At 85% germination, seeds = 32 ÷ 0.85 = 37.6, rounded up to 38 seeds. At 50% germination — common for older seed — the same bed needs 32 ÷ 0.50 = 64 seeds. That difference is why sowing "a few extra" without a number often leaves you short, while sowing the whole packet leaves you with dozens of seedlings you do not need.

What affects germination in practice

Seed age, storage temperature and moisture, sowing depth, soil temperature and watering all affect germination. Seed stored cool and dry lasts longer than seed left in a warm shed. Sowing too deep is a common cause of failure for small seeds, which need light or very shallow cover. A seed tray kept evenly moist, not soggy, germinates far better than one that dries out between waterings.

Frequently asked questions

Should I test old seed before sowing?

Yes, if it matters. Put 10 seeds on damp paper in a warm place and count how many sprout in a week. That gives a rough germination rate to enter in the calculator.

Why sow more than I need?

Because not every seed germinates and not every seedling survives transplanting. Thinning to the strongest seedlings gives better plants than sowing exactly the number you want.

Does the calculator work for direct sowing?

Yes, but use a lower germination rate for direct sowing, because weather, birds and soil crusting cause more losses than indoor sowing.

Understanding your results

The result has two parts: how many plants fit in your area, and how many seeds to sow to get those plants after germination losses. If the seed number looks much larger than the plant number, your germination rate is low — that is the calculator telling you the seed is the problem, not the area. Test old seed on damp paper before sowing a whole bed with it.

Can I use this calculator for trays instead of beds?

Yes, if you know the tray area and the spacing you will use when pricking out. Enter the tray area, not the bed area, and thin seedlings to final spacing after transplanting.

What germination rate should I use if the packet does not give one?

Use 70% for fresh seed from a reputable supplier, 50% for seed over a year old, and lower still for seed stored warm or damp. A 10-seed damp-paper test gives a better number than any guess.

Should I sow all the seeds the calculator gives?

Sow them, but thin to the strongest seedlings. Sowing fewer and hoping is worse — a thin stand cannot be fixed later, while excess seedlings can be thinned or transplanted.
